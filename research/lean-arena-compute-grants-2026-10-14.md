# Sponsored compute for Lean Refactor Arena

Primary sources checked live, 2026-10-14. No applications or contacts sent.

## Model route and parity limits

- **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash already has public MIT-licensed weights.** [Official checkpoint and model card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash). Public weights alone do not establish compliance with the competition hardware/time envelope.
- [Custom A100 runtime](https://github.com/shi3z/deepseekv4.1-A100-custom) uses packed FP4 experts, FP8 dense weights and host-RAM Engram tables. It advertises four-card profiles, but several README performance figures originate from a five-GPU server and example scripts reference an abliterated checkpoint. Use official weights and measure the selected four-card profile; do not assume parity with the provider API.
- [First-hand independent deployment report](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash/discussions/43) loaded the official checkpoint on 4xA100 80GB NVLink with 251GiB host RAM, cold loading in 43m19s. It demonstrated only 4096-token context with almost no spare GPU VRAM and explicitly called that configuration impractical for large-context agentic coding. This validates loading, not our harness or a 48h full benchmark.
- **Cheaper fallback:** [Qwen3.8-27B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B), or a Lean-specialist [Goedel-Prover-V2-32B](https://huggingface.co/Goedel-LM/Goedel-Prover-V2-32B). Approximate BF16 weight storage is 54GB for 27B and 64GB for 32B, before cache/runtime overhead; one A100 80GB may support a bounded-context pilot. No measured Arena parity is established.
- Current workspace `docs/RESULTS-v6-pilot.md` records DS4.1 local scores 86.07/88.25/86.44 versus Qwen3.8 Flash API scores 86.02/87.32/78.43, with the last Qwen run interrupted. The API comparison cannot be transferred to Qwen3.8-27B.

## Best routes

1. **CloudRift AI Grant: strongest confirmed unaffiliated route.** $100–$1,000 GPU credits; expressly accepts independent researchers, indie developers, students and hobbyists. Application asks project description, required GPUs and links; prior work helps but is not required. Reviews every two weeks (2nd/4th Tuesdays); mention CloudRift when publishing. Emmy compiler projects receive priority, but other useful open projects qualify. [Official grant](https://www.cloudrift.ai/ai-grant). Its [A100 page](https://www.cloudrift.ai/a100) lists A100 **80GB SXM4** from **$1.05/GPU-hour**, nodes up to 8 GPUs over NVLink. Inference: 4 cards cost at least $4.20/node-hour, so $1,000 covers about **238 node-hours**, or ~5 full 48-hour runs before extra costs. $100 covers ~24 hours. Exact available 4-card node, actual billed rate, extras and grant approval remain unconfirmed.

2. **Lambda Research Grant: up to $5,000 credits.** Official page has active application link and states support for “qualifying researchers,” plus possible website feature. It does not publicly spell out independent researcher eligibility or turnaround; do not assume university affiliation is mandatory or approval assured. [Official research page](https://lambda.ai/research), [application form](https://form.typeform.com/to/Wn8YWMIj). Ask specifically for 4x80GB A100 or an allowed alternative and public reproducibility work. Reasoning/competition work has precedent: page describes Lambda-sponsored ARC Prize teams.

3. **Modal academic credits: up to $10k, if academic eligibility fits.** [Program](https://modal.com/academics), [pricing eligibility](https://modal.com/pricing) describes graduate students, labs and researchers. Independent acceptance not confirmed. Program page links an academic credits/email form and grant updates. [GPU docs](https://modal.com/docs/guide/gpu) explicitly support `A100-80GB:4`, up to 8 same-physical-machine GPUs; requests above 2 GPUs may wait longer. A payment method is normally required. Starter's $30 monthly free credits are useful only for a small smoke experiment, not sustained full benchmark runs. Their ARC Prize researcher testimonial makes formal-reasoning compute a plausible pitch, not evidence of eligibility.

4. **Competition organizers: practical first ask, unconfirmed offer.** Official [competition application source](https://huggingface.co/spaces/delta-lab-ai/lean-refactor-arena/blob/main/app.py) lists AWS as technical sponsor and Amazon Automated Reasoning as financial sponsor. No public participant compute-grant promise found. Ask organizers whether sponsor credits, a shared inference endpoint or a short reserved 4xA100 reproduction allocation exist. Frame request as 4x80GB A100 for 48h (=192 GPU-hours) plus a bounded pilot; include existing score, reproducible harness and public code. Do not present sponsorship as guaranteed.

## India: subsidized rather than free

[IndiaAI Compute portal](https://compute.indiaai.gov.in/login) has research, students and early-stage researcher categories. Its current [price list](https://compute.indiaai.gov.in/pricelist) explicitly lists **A100.80GB.4x, 320GB total, ₹543.6/hour** on demand. A 48h node run is **₹26,092.8** before extras; if approved for 40% subsidy, compute remainder is **₹15,655.68**. Do not use the monthly reserved hourly figure for a two-day job.

[End-user policy](https://compute.indiaai.gov.in/files/end-user-policy.pdf) explicitly includes independent AI researchers. Main researcher eligibility is h-index ≥5 OR an AI/allied journal paper with impact factor >2.5 OR consolidated 150 citations. Early researchers require credentials/work profile, APAAR and institute/research-guide/IndiaAI CEO endorsement. Subsidy requests require PMEC review; requests 1–25th of month, result by 10th next month. Policy contains 0–50% language in one section but **maximum 40%** in its subsidy section, matching government messaging; conservatively budget at most 40% and confirm quote. Users pay costs above subsidy. Therefore helpful with cash or lab support; not a zero-cash path.

## Routes to avoid overclaiming

- **Hugging Face community GPU grants** exist for public innovative Spaces. [Official docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/en/spaces-gpus) list 4xA100 320GB hardware and grant application in Space Settings. That proves both exist, **not** that grants include four A100s or private/batch competition workloads. Best use: public proof-refactoring demo request, with exact hardware separately approved. ZeroGPU is not a four-card persistent cluster.
- **NVIDIA academic grants**: [current official program](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/industries/higher-education-research/academic-grant-program/) says not accepting applications; applicant must be full-time faculty at accredited PhD-granting institute. Not immediate independent route.
- **RunPod**: old `/research-credits` now resolves to generic marketing. [Academic page](https://www.runpod.io/academic-research) promises tailored billing, not free credits. [Startup program](https://www.runpod.io/startup-program) is selective for venture-backed startups; starter mentions $1,000 but eligibility would need qualification. No confirmed general independent research grant found.
- **Ai2**: public open infrastructure announcements do not establish a currently open unrestricted independent multi-GPU allocation application. Do not promise access.

Competition open-model budget is a hardware/time envelope: at most **4x80GB A100** and **48h full benchmark**, not necessarily a requirement to own/rent four cards continuously. Training and final reproduction resources may be separate; verify organizer rules before substituting faster GPUs for scored inference.
